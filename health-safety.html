Health & Safety

Last updated: 22 October 2026

STILL supports calm and relaxation. It is not medical care. Please read this before you breathe with STILL.

1. General wellness only

STILL offers breathing exercises, sound, mood check-ins, and reflection for relaxation and general wellness. It is not a medical device. It does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any condition, and it does not give medical advice. The mood check-ins and progress figures are for your own reflection and are not a clinical assessment.

2. Not a substitute for professional care

STILL does not replace a doctor, therapist, or other healthcare professional. If you have a health concern, or if symptoms continue, speak to a qualified professional. Do not delay or stop treatment because of anything in the App.

3. Breathe gently and listen to your body

Breathe at a pace that feels comfortable. Never force your breath, strain, or hold your breath longer than is comfortable. The timing in an exercise is a guide, not a target.

  • You can pause, resume, or leave any session at any time.
  • Breath holding is optional. Box Breathing and 4-7-8 Breathing include holds. Coherent Breathing, Extended Exhale, and the Gentle rhythm have none. You can turn holds off in Settings (Breath holding).
  • Custom Breathing lets you choose your own timings, including long inhales and holds. Start with short, easy timings and lengthen them slowly, only if it stays comfortable.
  • Stop and breathe normally if you feel dizzy, lightheaded, short of breath, tingling, anxious, or unwell.

4. Choose a safe moment

Do not use STILL while driving, riding, operating machinery, swimming, bathing, or doing any activity that needs your attention. Sit or lie down in a safe, comfortable place. Reminders are gentle prompts: do not act on them while doing something that requires focus.

5. Check with a professional first if

  • You have a heart, lung, or breathing condition such as asthma or COPD.
  • You have high or low blood pressure, epilepsy, or a history of fainting.
  • You are pregnant.
  • You live with panic attacks or an anxiety disorder that breathing exercises can trigger.

Ask a healthcare professional before you use exercises that include breath holds.

6. When to stop and get help

Stop the exercise at once if you feel faint, have chest pain, severe shortness of breath, or any sudden or worrying symptom. If it does not pass quickly, or if you are worried, seek medical help.

7. Sound, motion, and vibration

Keep the volume at a comfortable level. If animations bother you, choose Reduced under Motion in Settings. Gentle vibration can be affected by your device settings.

8. In an emergency

STILL is not an emergency or crisis service. If you or someone else is in danger, call your local emergency number straight away: 112 in the European Union, 15 (SAMU) in France. In France, the national suicide-prevention line is 3114 (free, 24 hours a day). Outside France, contact your local emergency or crisis service.
